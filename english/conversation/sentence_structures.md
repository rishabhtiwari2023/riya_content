# Advanced Sentence Structures

## Complex Sentence Patterns

### 1. Conditional Sentences (If-clauses)

**First Conditional (Real possibility)**
- Structure: If + present simple, will + base verb
- *Example: If you study regularly, you will improve quickly.*
- *Example: If she arrives early, we will start the meeting.*

**Second Conditional (Hypothetical)**
- Structure: If + past simple, would + base verb
- *Example: If I had more time, I would practice speaking daily.*
- *Example: If he knew the answer, he would tell us.*

**Third Conditional (Past hypothetical)**
- Structure: If + past perfect, would have + past participle
- *Example: If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.*
- *Example: If they had left earlier, they would have caught the train.*

### 2. Relative Clauses

**Defining Relative Clauses**
- *Example: The employee who works hardest often gets promoted.*
- *Example: This is the report that was mentioned in the meeting.*
- *Example: The office where we work is downtown.*

**Non-defining Relative Clauses**
- *Example: My colleague, who has been here for ten years, is retiring.*
- *Example: The project, which took six months to complete, was successful.*
- *Example: London, where I studied, is a vibrant city.*

### 3. Passive Voice Constructions

**Present Passive**
- *Example: The reports are prepared by the finance team every month.*
- *Example: English is spoken in many countries around the world.*

**Past Passive**
- *Example: The proposal was rejected by the committee.*
- *Example: The documents were submitted before the deadline.*

**Future Passive**
- *Example: The results will be announced next week.*
- *Example: New policies will be implemented in January.*

**Modal Passive**
- *Example: The issue should be addressed immediately.*
- *Example: This matter must be discussed with management.*

### 4. Reported Speech

**Statements**
- Direct: "I am working on the project."
- Reported: *She said that she was working on the project.*

**Questions**
- Direct: "Where is the meeting?"
- Reported: *He asked where the meeting was.*

**Commands**
- Direct: "Please submit your reports."
- Reported: *The manager told us to submit our reports.*

### 5. Participle Clauses

**Present Participle (-ing)**
- *Example: Knowing the importance of the task, she worked extra hours.*
- *Example: Having completed the training, they were ready to start.*

**Past Participle**
- *Example: Written in simple language, the manual was easy to understand.*
- *Example: Impressed by her presentation, the clients approved the proposal.*

### 6. Inversion for Emphasis

**Negative adverbials**
- *Example: Never have I seen such dedication.*
- *Example: Rarely does he make mistakes.*
- *Example: Only after the meeting did I understand the problem.*

**Conditional inversion**
- *Example: Had I known earlier, I would have prepared differently.*
- *Example: Should you need any help, please contact me.*

### 7. Cleft Sentences (for emphasis)

**It-cleft**
- *Example: It was John who suggested this approach.*
- *Example: It is communication skills that matter most.*

**What-cleft**
- *Example: What I need is more practice.*
- *Example: What surprised me was the positive response.*

### 8. Parallel Structure

**Balanced sentences**
- *Example: She is responsible for planning, organizing, and executing projects.*
- *Example: The company values innovation, integrity, and excellence.*
- *Example: To succeed, you must work hard, stay focused, and never give up.*

### 9. Subordinate Clauses

**Time clauses**
- *Example: Before you leave, please ensure all tasks are completed.*
- *Example: While I was reviewing the data, I noticed an error.*
- *Example: As soon as the budget is approved, we will proceed.*

**Reason clauses**
- *Example: Since it was raining heavily, the event was postponed.*
- *Example: Because she arrived late, she missed the introduction.*
- *Example: As he had experience, he was chosen for the role.*

**Concession clauses**
- *Example: Although the task was difficult, they completed it on time.*
- *Example: Despite being tired, she continued working.*
- *Example: Even though prices increased, demand remained high.*

### 10. Subjunctive Mood

**Formal suggestions/requirements**
- *Example: It is essential that he be present at the meeting.*
- *Example: I suggest that she take the training course.*
- *Example: The policy requires that all employees submit monthly reports.*

## Practice Exercises

**Combine these simple sentences into complex ones:**

1. Simple: The meeting started. Everyone was present.
   Complex: *The meeting started when everyone was present.*

2. Simple: She studied hard. She wanted to improve.
   Complex: *She studied hard because she wanted to improve.*

3. Simple: The report is comprehensive. It was prepared by our team.
   Complex: *The comprehensive report was prepared by our team.*
